# War

## Summary

War is Machiavelli's subject and barely a subject for the other two. For him the chief foundations of all states are "good laws and good arms"; a prince should select nothing "else for his study, than war and its rules and discipline"; mercenaries are "valiant before friends, cowardly before enemies", "all armed prophets have conquered", and "war is not to be avoided", only put off to another's advantage. Marcus Aurelius, who wrote on campaign, has almost nothing on it: war supplies an image of duty done "like a soldier in the assault on a town", a reminder that the great conquerors died like everyone else, and one dry judgement on those proud of having taken Sarmatians — "Are not these robbers". Nietzsche moves war inward and into breeding: "Under peaceful conditions the militant man attacks himself", the man of mixed inheritance has a war in him that he wants ended, a "new warlike age" may strengthen skepticism, and constant struggle is what forms a severe, "warlike" type.
