# Law

## Summary

For Marcus Aurelius law is cosmic before it is civic: reason is common, so "there is a common law also; if this is so, we are fellow-citizens"; "Law rules all", "the law is master, and he who breaks the law is a runaway", and what does not harm law harms neither state nor citizen. Machiavelli treats laws as an instrument resting on force: the foundations of states are "good laws and good arms", and "there cannot be good laws where the state is not well armed"; contest by the law is proper to men but not enough, conquered provinces stay quiet when their laws are left unaltered, and a new prince wins honour by founding new ones. Nietzsche turns on Marcus's premise — the Stoics read "the canon of your law in Nature" only by dictating it, and "Nature's conformity to law" is interpretation — and puts law in the will: genuine philosophers are "commanders and lawgivers", and "all law rests on this double reverence" for age and tradition.
