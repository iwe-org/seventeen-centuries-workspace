# Friendship

## Summary

Marcus Aurelius treats friendship as a matter of character learned from particular men: "how to receive from friends what are esteemed favours", "not to be indifferent when a friend finds fault", Antoninus's disposition "to keep his friends, and not to be soon tired of them"; even those who blame him are "by nature" friends, and "Nothing is more disgraceful than a wolfish friendship". Machiavelli's friends are supporters and allies, valued by their reliability: "friendships that are obtained by payments, and not by greatness or nobility of mind" fail in need, a prince who is well armed "will have good friends", he must keep the people friendly, and he is respected when "a true friend or a downright enemy". Nietzsche writes of friendship from solitude: "the good friends" think friendship gives "a right to ease", a profound man lives as a mask "in the hearts and heads of his friends", noble morality needs enemies in order to befriend well, and the closing poem asks "Where linger ye, my friends?"
