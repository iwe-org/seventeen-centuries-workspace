# Opinion

## Summary

The three authors mean different things by the word. For Marcus Aurelius opinion is the judgement one adds to events, and it is the one thing fully ours: "everything is opinion, and opinion is in thy power"; "Take away thy opinion, and then there is taken away the complaint". What others think counts for little — Socrates called the opinions of the many "bugbears to frighten children". For Machiavelli opinion is exactly what others think, and it is a prince's working material: "few really know what you are", and those few dare not oppose "the opinion of the many", so reputation must be managed. Nietzsche judges opinions by their use for life — "The falseness of an opinion is not for us any objection to it" — treats waiting on others' good opinion as vanity and a slave's instinct, and lists public opinion among the commanders the herd obeys.
