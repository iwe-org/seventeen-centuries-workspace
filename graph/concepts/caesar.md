# Caesar

## Summary

Marcus Aurelius names Caesar only to diminish him: "Alexander, and Pompeius, and Caius Caesar", after destroying whole cities and cutting down tens of thousands, departed from life like everyone else, and set beside Diogenes, Heraclitus and Socrates such men were slaves to how many things. Machiavelli uses him as a working example: Caesar imitated Alexander as every great man takes a model, and he reached empire by liberality — but only as one "who wished to become preeminent in Rome", spending what was others'; had he survived and not moderated his expenses he would have destroyed his government. Nietzsche turns the name into a type. Where opposed instincts in a mixed age produce not weakness but mastery, there arise those "predestined for conquering and circumventing others", of whom Alcibiades and Caesar are the finest examples; and he gives the genuine philosopher the same stamp, the "Caesarian trainer and dictator of civilization", in contrast to the merely objective scholar.
