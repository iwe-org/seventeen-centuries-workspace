# Self-Mastery

## Summary

Self-mastery is the centre of Marcus Aurelius's practice: "check desire; extinguish appetite; keep the ruling faculty in its own power", so that one is no longer "pulled by the strings like a puppet" and the mind "free from passions is a citadel". Machiavelli touches it only as prudence: a prince should "proceed in a temperate manner" and abstain from his subjects' property and women so as not to be hated — and he doubts how far anyone can rule himself, since a man "cannot deviate from what nature inclines him to do". Nietzsche both suspects and prizes it. He treats Stoic self-command as a tyranny over oneself and says the will to overcome an emotion is "only the will of another" emotion; yet the noble man honours whoever "has power over himself", and "the discipline of great suffering" is for him what has raised mankind.
