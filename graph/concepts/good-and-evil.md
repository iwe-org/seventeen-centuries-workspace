# Good and Evil

## Summary

Marcus Aurelius narrows good and evil to what lies in our own power: "nothing is either bad or good which can happen equally to the bad man and the good", so pain, death and change are no evils, and wrongdoers act from "ignorance of what is good and evil". Machiavelli keeps the ordinary meanings but refuses to let them bind a ruler: a man who is good in everything is ruined "among so much that is evil", so the prince should try "not to diverge from the good if he can avoid doing so" yet know how to do otherwise, and prudence is to "take the lesser evil". Nietzsche questions the opposition itself. He traces "good" and "bad" to the rulers and "good" and "evil" to the ruled, for whom the evil man is the one who "arouses fear", and he places love, greatness and his own philosophy "beyond good and evil".
