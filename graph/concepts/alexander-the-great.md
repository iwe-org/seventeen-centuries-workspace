# Alexander the Great

## Summary

Marcus Aurelius invokes Alexander always as the great name that death and time have levelled: "Alexander the Macedonian and his groom by death were brought to the same state", his court was the same drama as any other with different actors, and compared with Diogenes, Heraclitus and Socrates the conqueror was a slave to many things; if he and Philip acted like tragedy heroes, no one has condemned Marcus to imitate them. Machiavelli treats him as a case to be explained and a model to be copied. He asks why the kingdom of Darius, once Alexander had beaten him in the field, stayed quiet under the successors, and answers from the kind of state it was, governed by one lord and his servants; he notes that Alexander "imitated Achilles" as Caesar imitated Alexander, and that he could be "a ready giver" of what belonged to others. Nietzsche has no passage on him.
