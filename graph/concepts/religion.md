# Religion

## Summary

For Marcus Aurelius religion is a disposition more than a cult: piety means being "content with the lot which is assigned to thee", prayer should ask not for outcomes but for freedom from fear and desire, and his model Antoninus was "religious" yet "without superstition"; lying and chasing pleasure count as impiety. Machiavelli treats religion as a force in politics: ecclesiastical principalities are "sustained by the ancient ordinances of religion", a prince must seem religious because there is "nothing more necessary to appear to have than this last quality", and Ferdinand succeeds by "always using religion as a plea". Nietzsche examines the religious life itself — the "religious neurosis", the saint, the ladder of religious cruelty — and, like Machiavelli, values it as an instrument: the philosopher will use it for "his disciplining and educating work", while religions that rule "paramountly" exact a terrible cost.
