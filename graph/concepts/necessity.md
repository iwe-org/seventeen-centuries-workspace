# Necessity

## Summary

Marcus Aurelius meets necessity as destiny woven into the whole: what happens was "apportioned and spun out to thee", so one should "Love that only which happens to thee and is spun with the thread of thy destiny"; only the rational animal can "follow voluntarily", while "simply to follow is a necessity imposed on all", and "If then there is an invincible necessity, why dost thou resist?" Machiavelli knows no cosmic fate here, only the pressure of circumstances that excuses and commands: a prince must learn to do wrong "according to necessity", is "very often forced to do evil", and "that war is just which is necessary". Nietzsche attacks the concept and then reclaims it: "necessity" and "non-free will" are our own fictions, yet the world has a "necessary" course, creators feel freest when they do "everything of necessity", and at bottom each of us carries "a granite of spiritual fate".
