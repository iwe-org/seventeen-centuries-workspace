# Nobility

## Summary

What is noble is the question of Nietzsche's last chapter. In ancient Greece the nobility called themselves "the truthful", and moral designations were first applied to men and only later to actions. What marks the noble is not actions, which are always ambiguous, nor works, but a fundamental certainty the noble soul has about itself — something that cannot be sought, found or lost: "the noble soul has reverence for itself." Elsewhere he calls loving mankind for God's sake the noblest and remotest sentiment men have attained, a love that gets its proportion and delicacy from a higher intention.
