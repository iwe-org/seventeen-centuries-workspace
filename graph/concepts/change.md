# Change and Time

## Summary

For Marcus Aurelius change is the law of the universe and a consolation: "Time is like a river", substance is "in a flux", and since "the universal nature delights in change", "Loss is nothing else than change" and nothing to fear. Machiavelli thinks of change politically. He notes that "time drives everything before it", that innovators are in danger, and that rulers fall because in quiet times they never expect a change; success belongs to whoever suits "the spirit of the times", but "fortune being changeful and mankind steadfast in their ways", few can turn with her. Nietzsche's change is historical and personal rather than cosmic: "an age of dissolution", a "turning-point of history" at which an old discipline gives way, Europeans and Germans still becoming — set against the wish to have the world again "for all eternity" and the artist's "Too late!". Marcus accepts change, Machiavelli tries to ride it, Nietzsche diagnoses it.
