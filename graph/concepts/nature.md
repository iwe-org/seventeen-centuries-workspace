# Nature

## Summary

Nature is Marcus Aurelius' norm and his cosmos at once: "nothing is evil which is according to nature", every "particular nature is a part" of "the common nature", the universal nature "delights in change" and brings each thing what is for its good, and to transgress it is impiety; conduct is summed up as acting "as thy nature leads thee" and enduring what the common nature brings. Machiavelli has no such whole; nature for him is disposition and tendency — the wish to acquire is "very natural and common", a prince must "make use of both natures", man's and beast's, and a man "cannot deviate from what nature inclines him to do". Nietzsche attacks the Stoic formula of living according to nature directly — "what fraud of words!" — since Nature is "boundlessly indifferent"; every morality is "a sort of tyranny against" nature, exploitation belongs to "the nature of the living being", and his own task is "to translate man back again into nature".
