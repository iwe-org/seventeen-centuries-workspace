# Solitude

## Summary

Marcus Aurelius moves solitude inward: men seek retreats in the country, by the sea and in the mountains, but "it is in thy power whenever thou shalt choose to retire into thyself", and the mind free from passions is a citadel; place is indifferent, so one may "live as on a mountain" anywhere, and the good man needs neither solitude nor much company. He draws a hard limit, though — whoever withdraws from the common reason, or hates his neighbour, is an abscess, a branch cut off from the tree. Machiavelli has no passage on it. Nietzsche prizes actual separation from the many: the free spirits are the "born, sworn, jealous friends of solitude", solitude is one of four virtues, "a sublime bent and bias to purity", and greatness now means standing alone. He also knows its cost — the compulsory recluse turns poisoner, and the man of knowledge must leave his citadel and go down among men.
