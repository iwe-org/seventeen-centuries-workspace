# Honesty and Deception

## Summary

Marcus Aurelius holds honesty absolute and simple: "If it is not right, do not do it: if it is not true, do not say it"; "he too who lies is guilty of impiety", and hypocrisy is a wrong the soul does to itself. Machiavelli praises integrity and then sets it aside for rulers: "a wise lord cannot, nor ought he to, keep faith when such observance may be turned against him", and "he who has known best how to employ the fox has succeeded best" — though the same prince must keep his servants honest and make men tell him the truth. Nietzsche is on both sides at once. Honesty is the virtue his free spirits cannot shed, yet "perhaps nobody has ever been sufficiently truthful" about truthfulness, deception is woven into life, and the profound need masks: every word is also a mask. Marcus forbids deceit, Machiavelli licenses it, Nietzsche examines why we want truth.
