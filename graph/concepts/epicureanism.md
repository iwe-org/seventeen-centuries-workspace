# Epicureanism

## Summary

Nietzsche reads Epicureanism less as a doctrine than as a symptom and a mask. For the weak man of a mixed age, at war with his own contradictory instincts, it is a soothing medicine: happiness as repose, undisturbedness and final unity. For those who have suffered deeply, "Epicurism" is one of the finer disguises — an ostentatious boldness of taste that takes suffering lightly and keeps sympathizing hands at a distance, so that the sufferer is misunderstood on purpose.
