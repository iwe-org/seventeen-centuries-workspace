# Asceticism

## Summary

Nietzsche treats asceticism from three sides. The ascetic himself has commanded the reverence of the mightiest men, who sensed in his voluntary privation a superior force testing itself — strength and will to power under another form — and suspected him of knowing some great danger. Ascetics also mark the second rung of religious cruelty, when men sacrificed to their God their strongest instincts, their "nature", with a festal joy that still shines in the ascetic's cruel glance. And asceticism, together with puritanism, is an almost indispensable means of educating and ennobling a race that wants to rise above its origins: religion offers the rising classes self-control, silence and solitude as a preparation for command.
