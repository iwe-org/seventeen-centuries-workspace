# Power

## Summary

For Marcus Aurelius the power that matters is inward: "opinion is in thy power", only "those things which are in our power" are good or bad, and no "tyrant" touches the understanding; outward rule is a danger — "Take care that thou art not made into a Caesar" — and real "strength" belongs to mildness. Machiavelli treats power as the prince's material, measured and husbanded: whether he can "support himself with his own resources", how the Church grew in "temporal power", why "he who is the cause of another becoming powerful is ruined", and why nothing is so unstable as "fame or power not founded on its own strength". Nietzsche turns power into the principle of everything living: life itself is "Will to Power", willing is a feeling of "supremacy", philosophy and even the will to truth are forms of it, and noble morality springs from "the feeling of plenitude, of Power, which seeks to overflow".
