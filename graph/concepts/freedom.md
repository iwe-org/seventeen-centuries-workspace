# Freedom

## Summary

The three authors mean three different things. For Marcus Aurelius freedom is inward and cannot be taken: "No man can rob us of our free will", the mind free from passions "is a citadel", one is to be "neither the tyrant nor the slave of any man", and even changing one's opinion under correction "is as consistent with freedom as it is to persist". For Machiavelli it is a political condition of cities — those "accustomed to live under their own laws and in freedom" never let the memory of their former liberty rest, and "Rome and Sparta stood for many ages armed and free" — with free will appearing only as the half of our actions fortune leaves us. Nietzsche doubts the word itself: freedom of the will is "the emotion of supremacy" over what must obey, independence is "a privilege of the strong", everything of the nature of freedom grew under "the tyranny of such arbitrary law", and the desire for freedom belongs "to slave-morals".
