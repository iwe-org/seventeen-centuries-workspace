# Christianity

## Summary

Marcus Aurelius mentions the Christians once, and only as a contrast: readiness to leave the body should come from a man's own judgement, "not from mere obstinacy, as with the Christians". Machiavelli treats Christianity almost wholly as the Church, a power among Italian powers: ecclesiastical principalities are sustained by the ancient ordinances of religion and can be held however their princes live, the French king's blunder was to aggrandize the Church, a prince must above all appear religious, and Ferdinand of Spain acted with "pious cruelty". Nietzsche makes Christianity a central object of his critique of values: the Christian faith is from the beginning sacrifice of freedom, pride and self-confidence of spirit; the Church sided with the sufferers until it bred a stunted European, "the most portentous of presumptions"; and the democratic movement is its inheritance. He also grants what it trained — discipline of spirit, reverence for the Bible, the sublimation of the sexual impulse into love.
