# Fear

## Summary

Marcus Aurelius treats fear as an error of judgement to be dissolved: one "afraid of an operation of nature" is "a child", change and death are nothing "to fear", he who fears "is a runaway", and the right prayer is for "the faculty of not fearing"; the only fit fear is "never to have begun to live according to nature". Machiavelli treats fear as an instrument of rule: "it is much safer to be feared than loved", injuries should leave no "fear of revenge", a prince has "two fears", from subjects and from foreign powers, and conspirators are checked by their own fear. Nietzsche treats fear as an origin of values: "fear is the mother of morals", moralities of prudence are "Morals as Timidity", piety is a product of "the *fear* of truth", and while slave morality's evil man "arouses fear", in master morality it is the good man who does.
