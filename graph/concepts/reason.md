# Reason

## Summary

Marcus Aurelius makes reason the centre of the self and of the world: the "ruling faculty" is "invincible" when self-collected, intelligence alone "is properly thine", reason is "common to him and the gods", and for the rational animal the same act is "according to nature and according to reason"; his recurring question is what use he is now making of it. Machiavelli has no doctrine of reason; he speaks only of practical intellect — the prince should "exercise the intellect" by reading histories, and there are "three classes of intellects", of which a prince needs at least the second. Nietzsche demotes it: "the greater part of conscious thinking must be counted among the instinctive functions", our reason "truckles to our strongest impulse", Christian faith is a "continuous suicide of reason", and against the line from Socrates to Descartes he concludes that "reason is only a tool".
