# The Romans

## Summary

For Marcus Aurelius "Roman" is a standard he holds himself to: "think steadily as a Roman and a man", a ruler at his post, though his city is Rome only "so far as I am Antoninus" and the world so far as he is a man. Machiavelli uses the Romans as his main school of statecraft: in the countries they annexed they sent colonies, humbled the powerful and, "foreseeing troubles, dealt with them at once"; they held Capua, Carthage and Numantia by dismantling them; Rome stood armed and free; and the empire's decline began with the enlisting of the Goths, while its emperors show what comes of having to satisfy the soldiers. Nietzsche's Rome is a type of noble values: its "noble, light-minded toleration" was what the oriental slave took revenge on through Christian faith, in the best period of the Romans a sympathetic action was neither good nor bad, and the Roman in the arena illustrates the enjoyment of cruelty.
