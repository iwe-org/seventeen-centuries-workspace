# God and the Gods

## Summary

Marcus Aurelius lives under gods he trusts: they "do exist, and they do care for human things", all that comes from them "is full of Providence", there is "one God who pervades all things", and "every man's intelligence is a god, and is an efflux of the deity"; even his doubts end in the rule to blame "neither gods" nor men and to pray only for a right mind. Machiavelli names God at the edges of his argument: Moses was "a mere executor of the will of God", church states are "exalted and maintained by God" and so not discussed, and in the closing appeal "God is not willing to do everything". Nietzsche treats God as a human conception to be dissected — "the father" in God "is thoroughly refuted", "God" and "Sin" will seem a child's plaything, men at last sacrifice "God for nothingness" — yet ends by praising Dionysus and supposing that "Gods also philosophize".
