# Soul

## Summary

For Marcus Aurelius the soul is what a man has to tend. It "turns and moves itself alone", is "dyed by the thoughts", and does violence to itself when it becomes "a tumour on the universe"; he asks whose soul he now has, calls himself "a little soul bearing about a corpse", and holds that one intelligent soul is distributed among rational beings and that souls, if they outlast the body, are in time diffused. Machiavelli has no passage on it. Nietzsche attacks the thing Marcus cultivates: the belief in the soul as "something indestructible, eternal, indivisible" ought to be expelled, the body is "a social structure composed of many souls", and modern philosophy is an attempt on the old conception by way of grammar. Yet he keeps the word for what he ranks — the history of the soul as the psychologist's hunting-domain, the widening of distance within the soul itself, and the certainty a noble soul has about itself.
