# Cesare Borgia

## Summary

Cesare Borgia is one of the few figures two of the books share, and they use him differently. Machiavelli presents him as the exemplary new prince: he acquired his state through the fortune of his father, Pope Alexander VI, yet showed extraordinary ability in securing it — crushing the factions, building forces of his own, giving the Romagna good government, and using cruelty to restore order before disowning its instrument. Only his father's death and his own illness undid him, and Machiavelli knows no better precepts for a new prince than his actions. Nietzsche, whose translator writes "Caesar Borgia", takes him as the type of the "man of prey": a tropical, thoroughly healthy human being whom moralists misunderstand when they look for something morbid in him, out of a preference for the temperate zones and the mediocre.
