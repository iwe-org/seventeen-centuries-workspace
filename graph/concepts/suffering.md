# Suffering

## Summary

Marcus Aurelius denies that suffering is an evil at all: pain belongs to the body, the ruling faculty need not add the opinion that it is bad, and of whatever befalls he says that "to bear it nobly is good fortune"; remove the judgement and the harm is gone, for nothing happens to a man which he is not formed by nature to bear. Machiavelli scarcely treats it: he complains in the dedication of his own "great and continued malignity of fortune", wants a prince to accustom his body to hardships, and paints Italy "beaten, despoiled, torn, overrun" to call for a liberator — suffering as a political condition, not a moral problem. Nietzsche reverses the Stoic indifference and the modern wish to abolish suffering alike: the herd's "deadly hatred of suffering" is what he opposes, since the discipline of great suffering has produced every elevation of man, and how deeply one can suffer almost determines rank.
