# Morality

## Summary

Marcus Aurelius has no theory of morality; "morals" for him are simply character — his own and other people's. He tells himself not to look at "the depraved morals of others", and defines the perfection of "moral character" as "passing every day as the last". Machiavelli opens a gap between morality and practice: "how one lives is so far distant from how one ought to live" that a prince who follows what "looks like virtue" finds it "would be his ruin", and he hesitates over whether "of evil it is possible to speak well". Nietzsche turns morality itself into the thing to be explained: "There is no such thing as moral phenomena, but only a moral interpretation of phenomena", systems of morals are a "sign-language of the emotions", and there are two basic types, master-morality and slave-morality. Marcus lives inside morality, Machiavelli brackets it for the state, Nietzsche puts it on trial.
