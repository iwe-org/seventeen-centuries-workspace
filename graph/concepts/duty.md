# Duty

## Summary

Duty in Marcus Aurelius is the plain daily word for what a rational, social being has to do, and it is the same for everyone and owed to everyone: "I do my duty: other things trouble me not", whether cold or warm, "like a soldier in the assault on a town"; when a man does wrong "It is thy duty then to pardon him", and one dies having discharged it. Machiavelli speaks of obligation only as a bond between men, and an unreliable one: love is kept "by the link of obligation which, owing to the baseness of men, is broken at every opportunity", a prince is not bound to keep faith with those who will not keep it, and men are bound "by the benefits they confer as much as by those they receive". Nietzsche keeps duty but makes it a mark of rank: "one has duties only to one's equals", and it is noble "never to think of lowering our duties to the rank of duties for everybody".
