# Rulers and Princes

## Summary

Marcus Aurelius writes as a ruler wary of rule: he thanks the gods for a father who showed that a man can live in a palace without guards or show, warns himself "Take care that thou art not made into a Caesar", and takes Antoninus as his model in everything. Machiavelli makes the prince's conduct a craft: war is "the sole art that belongs to him who rules", and the prince must learn to be feared without being hated, to keep or break faith as need requires, to choose servants and shun flatterers. Nietzsche asks who should rule at all: he sees a herd Europe that greets "an absolute ruler" such as Napoleon as a deliverance, calls the real philosophers "commanders and lawgivers", and names "the rearing of a new ruling caste for Europe" his serious topic. Marcus restrains the ruler, Machiavelli equips him, Nietzsche wants him bred.
