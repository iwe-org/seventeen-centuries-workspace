# Justice

## Summary

For Marcus Aurelius justice is the chief virtue and part of the order of things: "in justice the other virtues have their foundation", "He who acts unjustly acts impiously" and "acts unjustly to himself", right reason "differs not at all from the reason of justice", and even events happen "justly"; his rule is to do nothing "otherwise than as justice herself would act". Machiavelli mentions justice rarely and instrumentally: the people's aim is "more righteous" than the nobles', emperors who were "lovers of justice" mostly "came to a sad end", a victor must still "show some regard" to it, and "that war is just which is necessary". Nietzsche denies it any neutral standing: living itself is "being unjust", the herd finds punishment "somehow unfair", justice properly maintains "gradations of rank", and the noble soul says of its own egoism, "It is justice itself."
