# Life

## Summary

Marcus Aurelius measures life by its brevity and its present: "Of human life the time is a point", no man loses any life but the one he now lives, so every act should be done as if it were the last, and what remains is to live it according to nature — "where a man can live, there he can also live well". Life has value only through its use; its one fruit is a pious disposition and social acts, and three acts can be the whole drama. Machiavelli touches the question once, as a premise of method: "how one lives is so far distant from how one ought to live" that whoever follows the ought is ruined. Nietzsche makes life the standard by which judgements are tested: untruth is a condition of life, living is valuing and preferring, and life itself is will to power, "appropriation, injury, conquest of the strange and weak". Doctrines of equality or pity he reads as a will to the denial of life.
