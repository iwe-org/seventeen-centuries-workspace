# Pleasure and Pain

## Summary

Marcus Aurelius returns to pleasure and pain constantly, always to deny them standing: they "happen to good men and bad" alike and so are neither good nor evil, the ruling part is to stay undisturbed by "the movements in the flesh", "Pleasure then is neither good nor useful", and "he who pursues pleasure as good, and avoids pain as evil, is guilty of impiety"; pain that is intolerable carries us off and what lasts is tolerable. Machiavelli has no passage on it. Nietzsche agrees that they are no measure — every way of thinking that weighs things by them judges by "accompanying circumstances and secondary considerations" — but for the opposite reason: where Marcus wants the mind untouched, Nietzsche wants "the discipline of great suffering", finds a "superabundant enjoyment even in one's own suffering", and despises the wish to abolish suffering and Europe's "sickly irritability and sensitiveness towards pain".
