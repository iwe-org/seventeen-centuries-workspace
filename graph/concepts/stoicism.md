# Stoicism

## Summary

Marcus Aurelius states the school's tenets from the inside and names it only once, where things seem "even to the Stoics themselves" difficult to understand: for the rational animal the same act is according to nature and according to reason; only what is in our power is good or bad and the rest is indifferent; and "everything is opinion, and opinion is in thy power", so that removing the judgement removes the harm. Machiavelli has no passage on it. Nietzsche attacks exactly the first tenet — "what fraud of words!" — arguing that nature is boundlessly indifferent, that living is wanting to be otherwise than nature, and that the Stoics merely dictated their own morality to it. Elsewhere he treats Stoicism as one instance of the long constraint every morality imposes and as a recipe of coldness against the emotions, yet he borrows the name for his own honesty: "we latest Stoics".
