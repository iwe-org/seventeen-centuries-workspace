# Truth

## Summary

For Marcus Aurelius truth is a duty of speech and a property of the order of things: there is "one truth", a man should "always speak the truth freely and without disguise", "he too who lies is guilty of impiety", and he seeks "the truth by which no man was ever injured", while every soul "is involuntarily deprived of truth". Machiavelli wants "the real truth of the matter" rather than "the imagination of it", and that truth includes the uses of untruth: princes who did great things "held good faith of little account", though a prince must still let chosen advisers "tell you the truth". Nietzsche questions the value itself: "Supposing that Truth is a woman", why not rather untruth, since it is "nothing more than a moral prejudice that truth is worth more than semblance"; the strength of a mind is measured by how much truth "it could endure", and the philosophers' will to truth is Will to Power.
