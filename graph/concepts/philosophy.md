# Philosophy and Philosophers

## Summary

For Marcus Aurelius philosophy is the one guide of a life in flux — "One thing and only one, philosophy" — and it consists in keeping the daemon within unharmed; he returns to it as to a remedy for sore eyes, calls it mother beside the stepmother court, and insists that its work is "simple and modest", a matter of acting justly rather than of reputation or syllogisms. Machiavelli has no passage on it: his one use of the word is the epithet "Marcus the philosopher" in a list of emperors. Nietzsche turns the question on the philosophers themselves: every great philosophy is the confession of its author, philosophers are advocates of their prejudices, and philosophy is "this tyrannical impulse itself" that creates the world in its own image. Against the philosophical workers he sets the real philosophers, who are "commanders and lawgivers" and the bad conscience of their age — a legislating task where Marcus sought a discipline of obedience.
