# Flattery

## Summary

Marcus Aurelius treats flattery as a moral failing to be shunned on both sides: his adoptive father checked "popular applause and all flattery" and was "above flattery", no one is compelled by nature to flatter or to try to please men, praise from the many is a clapping of tongues, and even the gods "wish not to be flattered" but to be imitated. Machiavelli handles it as a practical danger of rule: flatterers are a pest "of whom courts are full", and since the only defence is to let men tell the truth, which costs respect when everyone may do it, the prince should choose a few wise men and allow them alone to speak freely, and only when asked. Nietzsche uses the word as a mark of rank and of bad philosophy: Epicurus called the Platonists flatterers of Dionysius, that is actors, and the noble type despises "the mendicant flatterers"; praise itself he finds obtrusive, noble only where one does not agree.
