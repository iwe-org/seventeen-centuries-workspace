# Meditations II.3

All that is from the [gods](../concepts/god.md) is full of Providence. That which is from [fortune](../concepts/fortune.md) is not separated from [nature](../concepts/nature.md) or without an interweaving and involution with the things which are ordered by Providence. From thence all things flow; and there is besides [necessity](../concepts/necessity.md), and that which is for the advantage of the whole universe, of which thou art a part. But that is good for every part of nature which the nature of the whole brings, and what serves to maintain this nature. Now the universe is preserved, as by the changes of the elements so by the changes of things compounded of the elements. Let these principles be enough for thee, let them always be fixed opinions. But cast away the thirst after books, that thou mayest not die murmuring, but cheerfully, truly, and from thy heart [thankful](../concepts/gratitude.md) to the gods.
